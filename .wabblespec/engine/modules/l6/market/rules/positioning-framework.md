# Positioning Framework

Rules for ICP definition, category selection, and positioning statement construction.

## ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

An ICP is not a demographic. It is a behavioral profile of the person or organization that gets the most value from the product and is most likely to buy.

ICP has five components:

| Component | What to define |
|---|---|
| Role | Job title or function (e.g., "senior backend engineer," "VP of Engineering") |
| Company type | Industry and business model (e.g., "B2B SaaS startup," "e-commerce retailer") |
| Company size | Headcount or revenue range where the problem is most acute |
| Key pain | The specific problem they are failing at today (one sentence, concrete) |
| Current alternative | What they use today instead — and why it falls short |

The ICP is wrong when it describes everyone. A product for "any developer" has no ICP. Force a specific definition, then expand after traction.

## Market category selection

Market category determines how the ICP evaluates and shortlists options. Three valid strategies:

**Existing category (compete):** Enter a known category (CRM, CI/CD, analytics) and position against the leader. Benefit: ICP already knows what they're buying. Risk: competing on the leader's terms.

**Re-segmented category:** Enter an existing category but define a new segment (e.g., "CRM for freelancers" or "CI/CD for ML workflows"). Benefit: clearer ICP match; less direct competition. Risk: educating the market on the segment.

**New category (create):** Name and define a new category. Benefit: become the category leader. Risk: expensive, slow — ICP has to learn the category exists before they can buy.

Default to re-segmented or existing category unless there is a clear reason to create a new one and the resources to educate the market.

## Positioning statement structure

Standard format (for internal alignment):

> For [ICP role] at [ICP company type/size] who [key pain], [product name] is the [market category] that [primary differentiated benefit]. Unlike [main competitive alternative], [product name] [key differentiator that makes the claim credible].

Rules:
- One sentence per section. No embellishment.
- The differentiator must be specific and verifiable, not generic
- "Unlike [alternative]" must name a real alternative the ICP actually uses

## Elevator pitch (external version)

30 seconds or less. Derived from the positioning statement:

> [Product name] helps [ICP role] [achieve specific outcome] without [the thing they hate about current alternatives]. [One proof point.]

Must end with a proof point (a metric, a customer name, a specific capability statement). Never end with a claim that cannot be substantiated.

## Validation test

A positioning statement passes if:
1. A member of the ICP, reading it, immediately recognizes their problem in it
2. A competitor cannot use the same statement without lying
3. The team can recite the core of it from memory
